I'm not an electronics engineer. Never was. This is the first thing I ever wired.

A device the size of a credit card listens, sends voice to a server, and makes an AI avatar speak. Total physical components: two.

## The hardware

An ESP32-WROOM-32 and an INMP441 microphone. The INMP441 is digital, speaks I2S natively, and connects with no resistors or extra capacitors.

| INMP441 | ESP32   |
|---------|---------|
| VDD     | 3.3V    |
| GND     | GND     |
| SD      | GPIO 34 |
| SCK     | GPIO 26 |
| WS      | GPIO 25 |
| L/R     | GND     |

L/R tied to GND forces the left channel. Enough for voice.

## The flow

The ESP32 captures audio in 512-sample buffers: 16kHz, mono, 16-bit. That stream goes over WebSocket to a Node.js server.

The server does three things in sequence:

1. **STT** — converts audio to text (Whisper)
2. **LLM** — generates a response based on the loaded context
3. **TTS + avatar** — synthesizes voice and animates the avatar

The avatar renders on a separate display — a tablet, a kiosk, or a monitor. The ESP32 only captures and transmits. Cheap hardware, server-side logic you can update without touching the device.

## Three contexts, same board

**Children**: the LLM receives instructions to use simple language and restrict topics. The avatar has a fixed personality — a character, a tutor. Latency from end of speech to avatar response: 1.2-1.8 seconds.

**Shopping center reception**: the avatar knows the store map, hours, and active promotions. No touchscreen, no assigned staff. Works in moderate background noise — the INMP441 has built-in far-field noise cancellation.

**Business**: acts as a front-desk receptionist or internal assistant. Takes messages, answers FAQs, routes queries. The LLM context changes per client; the hardware doesn't.

## What we learned

The bottleneck is not the board or the microphone. It's the accumulated latency across STT, LLM, and TTS. With all three via API, total time runs 2.5-3 seconds. With local STT (small Whisper model on the server) it drops below 1.5 seconds in most cases.

A cheap analog microphone breaks speech recognition. The INMP441 at 3.3V works reliably up to 2 meters in moderate background noise.
