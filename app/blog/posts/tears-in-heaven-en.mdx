You start out believing things get done on motivation. That if the project is worth it, the motivation will be there.

It doesn't work like that. Motivation comes and goes, and it doesn't warn you.

What's left at the end wasn't put there by your mood. It was put there by your conduct.

## States don't build

Emotions show up either way, you don't choose them. You'll have good weeks and weeks where nothing makes sense. That isn't up to you.

What is up to you is what you do in between.

Emotion is a state: it shows up, pushes for a while, and burns out. Anger lasts a few days, euphoria less. After that you're in the same place, unless you made something concrete while it lasted.

Conduct is what you do while the state passes, and it's the only thing that leaves anything behind. That asymmetry is the whole point: you can move forward without motivation, you can't move forward without conduct.

## The decision sets the conduct

A decision made seriously isn't a strong feeling. It's a rule of behavior: it defines what you do on the days you don't want to do anything.

## Knowing what you're after

All of this collapses if you don't know what you want. Conduct without direction is endurance, and endurance doesn't take you anywhere.

You don't need it written down. It's enough to recognize it when it shows up and rule out fast what it isn't. That alone saves years.

The rest is repetition: the same conduct many times, on days that look nothing alike.

## Feeling isn't enough

This holds for a project and it holds for two people.

There can be feeling to spare and nothing happens. Feeling doesn't choose, doesn't hold a decision, doesn't show up on a Wednesday to do what needs doing. It's a state, under the same rules as the rest.

What builds is both people choosing the same thing and behaving accordingly. Many times, including the days they don't feel it.

Without that it doesn't work, and it doesn't matter how much there was.

## What isn't yours to judge

The only conduct you can demand is your own.

Nobody is obliged to carry your process with you. Someone else's willingness to stay runs on the same rules as yours: it comes, it goes, it doesn't warn anyone. It isn't a debt and it isn't proof of anything.

You can't expect to be understood either. What orders your life can look like an excuse or an obsession to someone else, and they'll have their reasons for seeing it that way. Explaining it better doesn't change that. You do it anyway.

You can hold a decision and accept that someone doesn't hold it alongside you. Neither one is up for judgment.

---

The emotions will be there. It's fine that they are.

But what gets built is measured by what you did, not by how you felt while doing it.
